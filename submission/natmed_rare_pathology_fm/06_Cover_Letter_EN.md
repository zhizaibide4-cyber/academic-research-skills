**Submission of a Review: "Pathology foundation models for rare disease diagnosis: data-efficient adaptation, cross-site robustness and the evidence gap"**

[If applicable: Following our presubmission enquiry of [date] and your invitation of [date] (reference [number]), we are pleased to submit] the enclosed Review for consideration in *Nature Medicine*.

Rare diseases affect an estimated 263–446 million people worldwide, and histopathology is the diagnostic step for many rare tumours and for a subset of non-neoplastic rare diseases. Pathology foundation models are often presented as the answer to the shortage of labelled cases that defines these conditions. Our Review examines whether the evidence supports this claim.

The Review makes three contributions. First, it compares data-efficient adaptation strategies, from zero-shot prompting and case retrieval to parameter-efficient and full fine-tuning, according to the number of labelled cases they require. Second, it brings together quantitative evidence that current foundation models still encode site, scanner and staining signatures, and shows why rare diseases amplify the consequences. Third, it introduces an explicit five-level evidence-maturity scale and applies it disease by disease. This analysis shows that the first prospective evaluations in rare disease histopathology have now been reported, for central nervous system tumours and autoimmune hepatitis, but that no randomized or controlled study has yet demonstrated benefit to patients. We end with a minimum reporting checklist and eight priority questions for the field.

We believe the Review will interest the journal's broad readership of clinicians, pathologists, computational scientists, regulators and funders, because it distinguishes what has been demonstrated from what is only technically plausible.

The manuscript contains about 4,900 words of main text, 5 figures, 2 tables, 3 boxes and 123 references. Every reference has been checked against its DOI, arXiv record or trial registration, and preprints and conference abstracts are identified as such. The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission.
